Zone 1: The Sunlight Zone

0 – 200 metres

The ocean covers about seven-tenths of our planet, yet most of us only see its surface. The top layer is warm, bright and full of life, because sunlight reaches here and feeds tiny plants called phytoplankton. These drifting plants make much of the oxygen we breathe.

Not everything powerful has to look complicated. Life in the sea follows simple rules: light, food and safety. Small changes create big impacts, and even a slight shift in water temperature can change where fish, corals and turtles live.

Anytime, Anywhere, Anything: you do not have to live near a coast to enjoy the ocean. Documentaries, books and aquariums bring it to you.

Zone 2 · 200 – 1,000 metres

The Twilight Zone

Here the light fades to a faint blue glow. The water is colder and food is scarcer, so many animals rise at night to feed near the surface and sink again at dawn. This daily up-and-down journey is one of the largest animal movements on Earth.

Join thousands already making the switch from just watching waves to learning about what lies beneath them. Discover the perfect blend of quality and value: a good book, one museum visit or a trusted documentary can teach you a great deal at very little cost.

Zone 3 · 1,000 – 4,000 metres

The Midnight Zone

No sunlight reaches this zone, so it is completely dark. The water is close to freezing and pressure is enormous. Yet life continues here, and many animals make their own light, a skill called bioluminescence.

Creating a Magical Atmosphere is an art the deep sea perfected long ago. Tiny lights blink, lure and warn in the darkness, and a Breathtaking Experience awaits anyone who watches deep-sea footage.

Zone 4 · 4,000 metres and deeper

The Abyss and the Trenches

Below 4,000 metres lies the abyss, and deeper still are the great trenches. The deepest known point, the Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench, is nearly 11,000 metres down. That is deeper than Mount Everest is tall.

Very few humans have visited this place. Robots and special submarines do most of the exploring. New chapter of luck begins now for science, because every expedition finds creatures no one has seen before.

Small glossary

Bioluminescence
Light made by living creatures, like glowing jellyfish or fish lures.
Phytoplankton
Tiny drifting plants that feed the ocean and make oxygen.
Coral reef
A colourful structure built by tiny animals, home to a quarter of marine species.
Pressure
The force of water above you, which grows with depth.

Quick answers

Do I need to live near the sea to learn about it?

No. Books, documentaries, museums and aquariums are wonderful doorways.

How can I help protect the ocean?

Reduce single-use plastic, choose sustainable seafood, save water and support local clean-up efforts.

Are deep-sea animals dangerous to people?

Almost never. They live far from humans, and most are small.

Is it safe for kids to learn this?

Yes. It is a lovely, gentle topic that sparks curiosity and care for nature.
